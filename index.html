India’s 121 Languages

An interactive view of India's major language entries by reported mother-tongue speakers, based on the Census of India 2011.
What are Scheduled and Unscheduled Languages?
Scheduled Languages are the languages included in the Eighth Schedule of the Constitution of India. The Eighth Schedule currently contains 22 languages.

When the Constitution came into force in 1950, the Eighth Schedule contained 14 languages. These were: Assamese, Bengali, Gujarati, Hindi, Kannada, Kashmiri, Malayalam, Marathi, Odia, Punjabi, Sanskrit, Tamil, Telugu and Urdu.

The list was subsequently expanded through Constitutional Amendments:

21st Constitutional Amendment, 1967
Sindhi was added.

71st Constitutional Amendment, 1992
Konkani, Manipuri and Nepali were added.

92nd Constitutional Amendment, 2003
Bodo, Dogri, Maithili and Santali were added.

96th Constitutional Amendment, 2011
The name "Oriya" was changed to "Odia".

This brought the Eighth Schedule to its present 22 languages.

Unscheduled Languages in this visualization are mother-tongue entries recorded in the Census that are not among the 22 languages listed in the Eighth Schedule.

“Unscheduled” does not mean unofficial, unrecognized, or less important.
What is the source and methodology?
The speaker figures are based on the Census of India 2011, Table C-16: Population by Mother Tongue, published by the Office of the Registrar General & Census Commissioner, India.

The figures represent people who reported the respective language as their mother tongue during the Census.

This visualization presents 121 language entries from the Census data. The 22 languages identified as Scheduled correspond to the languages included in the Eighth Schedule of the Constitution.

The remaining entries are classified here as Unscheduled for the purpose of this visualization.

Bubble size represents the relative number of reported mother-tongue speakers.

Ranking and bar lengths use the same speaker figures and are ordered from the largest reported mother-tongue population to the smallest.

The language-family classification and principal-area information shown in the visualization are provided as contextual categorization and should not be interpreted as exclusive geographic boundaries.

Primary source:
Census of India 2011 — Table C-16, Population by Mother Tongue.
